# Insights & Recommendations
**Call Center Performance Analysis | 2021 | 528,107 calls, 551 customer surveys**

## 1. Executive summary

**Service improved in Q4, but every call became 41% longer.** That adds about 5,191 agent hours in one quarter. Customers whose issue was not resolved on the first call are the least satisfied.

- **Volume and speed:** 492,391 of 528,107 calls were answered (PCA 93.2%). 35,716 calls (6.8%) were abandoned. Only 76.2% were answered within the service level (SLA), so about 1 in 4 calls did not get a fast answer.
- **Not stable across the year:** Q2 and Q3 were the weakest (SLA 69.1% and 70.2%; abandon rate 9.3% and 9.7%). Q4 was the best (SLA 83.1%, PCA 96.3%).
- **Longer calls in Q4:** AHT rose from 398 to 561 seconds. Talk time grew by 112 seconds and hold time tripled.
- **Teams are similar:** the AR and ENG median AHT differ by about 11 seconds (2.6%). Language is not the driver; the time period is.
- **Customers are satisfied, and resolution is the key:** CSAT 4.63 out of 5 and FCR 93.5%. Customers resolved on the first call scored 4.79; the others scored 2.39.

## 3. Page by page

### 3.1 Overview
**What the data shows**

- SLA is 76.2% and PCA is 93.2%. 89,995 calls were answered late and 35,716 were abandoned.
- Complaints are 51% of volume. Complaints peak in March and June; inquiries peak in January and May.
- PCA is similar by skill: Complain 92.7%, Inquiry 93.8%.

**Why it matters:** each 1 point of PCA is about 5,281 customers who were answered instead of lost.

**Recommendations**

1. Reduce abandoned calls in busy periods: offer a callback option and prioritize complaints.
2. Plan staffing by skill and by month, and cross-train agents, because complaint and inquiry peaks alternate.
3. Agree a service level target with the business and track it weekly.

### 3.2 Call Volume
**What the data shows**

- Average demand is about 1,447 calls per day. June (1,704) and February (1,655) are the busiest months; July (1,276) and December (1,284) are the quietest.
- Sunday and Monday are the busiest days (about 1,533); Friday is the lowest (about 1,153), roughly 25% below Monday. Weekends are not low, so the center runs 7 days.
- Daily volume in the second half of the year is 14% lower than in the first half.

**Why it matters:** with flat schedules, Fridays and the second half are overstaffed, while Sundays, Mondays and the June peak are understaffed.

**Recommendations**

1. Shift schedules: more agents on Sunday and Monday, fewer on Friday.
2. Add capacity for the June peak and use quiet months for training and leave.
3. Ask the business why the second half is lower (seasonality, campaigns, fixed issues) before forecasting.

### 3.3 Efficiency
**What the data shows**

- AHT by quarter: 398 / 442 / 442 / 561 seconds. Both language teams move together.
- The Q4 increase of 162 seconds per call is 69% talk time and 31% hold time. Hold time went from 25 to 76 seconds.
- Q4 also had the best service level, so the center traded longer calls for better service.
- Daily AHT boxplot: median 453 s (AR) and 442 s (ENG), with almost fully overlapping boxes. The time effect is about 25 times larger than the language effect.
- 14 outliers: 11 are days with 1 to 6 answered calls (statistical noise). 3 are real: 12, 13 and 16 November, when both teams had very high AHT. That points to a center-wide event.

**Why it matters:** the extra Q4 handle time is about 5,191 agent hours in one quarter. Every second saved on average AHT is worth about 137 agent hours per year.

**Recommendations**

1. Investigate Q4 hold time and the November week: system logs, call reasons, transfers and new agents.
2. Track talk, hold and after-call work weekly, with an alert when hold time passes its normal level.
3. Improve the knowledge base and the escalation path to reduce hold time.
4. Do not set separate AHT targets by language, and flag days with very few calls in reports.

### 3.4 Customer Experience
**What the data shows**

- CSAT is 4.63 and FCR is 93.5%. CSAT by quarter stays between 4.62 and 4.64.
- Months with higher FCR tend to have higher CSAT (correlation 0.53, a moderate positive relationship).
- Customers resolved on the first call scored 4.79 (97% satisfied or very satisfied). Unresolved customers scored 2.39 (58% dissatisfied).

**Why it matters:** an unresolved issue means a repeat call and the least satisfied customer.

**Caution:** correlation is not causation. Only 551 surveys (0.11% of answered calls), only 36 "No" answers, and abandoned callers cannot respond.

**Recommendations**

1. Call back customers who answered "No" on first call resolution (for example within 24 hours).
2. Run a first call resolution program: more knowledge and authority for agents, and track repeat contacts.
3. Improve the survey: more responses, record skill and language, and follow up with abandoned callers.

## 5. Hypotheses to validate
These are possible explanations, not facts. The data cannot confirm them.
- **Q4 hold time and talk time:** system slowness, more complex calls, new agents, or more time per call because of lower volume.
- **Q2 and Q3 weak service:** workload did not explain it (Q3 had the lowest workload but SLA 70.2%), so staffing or scheduling is a likely factor. Staffing data is needed.
- **Friday and second-half decline:** weekly day off and seasonality or campaigns.

## 6. Assumptions and limitations
- No targets were provided with the data, so periods are compared with each other and no targets were invented.
- The service level time threshold is not specified in the data.
- A missing day/skill/language row is treated as no calls (1,028 rows out of 1,460 possible combinations).
- No staffing or call reason data is available.
- CSAT has no skill or language, so those filters do not affect it. The survey covers 0.11% of answered calls.
- The boxplot uses daily AHT per team. Days with very few calls are unstable; excluding days under 30 answered calls does not change the conclusion.
